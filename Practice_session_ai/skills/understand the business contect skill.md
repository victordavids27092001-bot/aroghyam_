# Understand the Business Context — UX Discover Skill

## Purpose

Use this skill during **Phase 01 — Discover** to create a clear, evidence-aware understanding of Arogyam’s business context from a senior UX perspective. The outcome should help the team plan relevant user research without treating business assumptions as verified user needs.

## Product Context

Arogyam is an Indian integrative-wellness application intended to connect users with verified Ayurveda and Siddha practitioners, evidence-based food and nutrition education, supervised diet plans, wellness activities, reviewed traditional remedies, nearby facilities, medicine ordering and delivery, authenticated traditional medicinal products, laboratory services, and consent-based health records.

India is the primary market, with additional attention to Tamil Nadu, Siddha medicine, Tamil-language access, regional food practices, and rural, semi-urban, and urban users.

## Role

Act as a senior UX researcher, service designer, healthcare product strategist, and business-context analyst.

Apply these UX abilities:

- Stakeholder interviewing
- Business and user-goal alignment
- Assumption mapping
- Stakeholder and service-ecosystem mapping
- Value-proposition analysis
- Service and journey thinking
- Research-question formation
- Constraint and dependency analysis
- Risk identification
- Accessibility and inclusion awareness
- Healthcare evidence, safety, privacy, and regulatory awareness
- Traceable documentation and research handoff

## Required Inputs

Read, when available:

1. `../Input/aroghyam.brd.md`
2. `../phase-01 discover/phase-01 discover.md`
3. Existing stakeholder notes, business plans, budgets, timelines, technical documents, partner information, research, and policies supplied by the user

Treat these documents as context. Do not assume every statement is accurate, feasible, legally approved, or validated by users.

## Objective

Build a structured understanding of:

1. Why Arogyam is being created
2. Business objectives
3. Intended services
4. Target geography
5. Available resources
6. Technical and operational constraints
7. Healthcare, safety, and regulatory risks

Convert the analysis into useful UX research questions, assumptions to test, stakeholder needs, constraints, and decisions for the rest of Discover.

## Workflow

### 1. Understand why Arogyam is being created

Identify:

- The user or market problem the founders believe exists
- The intended change in the user’s current experience
- Why the problem matters now
- Who experiences the problem and in what context
- Existing alternatives and workarounds
- The organization’s motivation
- The consequences of building the wrong solution

UX questions:

- Is the stated problem expressed as a user problem or only as a business opportunity?
- What evidence currently supports it?
- Which parts are assumptions?
- Are safety, trust, accessibility, and cultural relevance part of the original purpose?

### 2. Understand business objectives

Identify short-, medium-, and long-term objectives, including:

- User value and intended outcomes
- Adoption and retention goals
- Trust and service-quality goals
- Consultation, subscription, medicine, laboratory, or partnership revenue goals
- Geographic expansion goals
- Social-impact or access goals
- Partner and practitioner goals
- Compliance and risk-reduction goals

For each objective, record:

- Business owner
- Intended user benefit
- Evidence or assumption
- Measurement approach
- Possible conflict with user welfare
- UX implication

Do not accept engagement, consultation volume, medicine sales, or test bookings as success by themselves. Include safety, comprehension, accessibility, informed choice, service quality, and trust guardrails.

### 3. Understand intended services

Create a service inventory covering, where proposed:

- Practitioner discovery and verification
- Online and in-person consultations
- General food and nutrition education
- Professionally supervised diet plans
- Yoga, sleep, breathing, movement, and stress-management activities
- Traditional and home-remedy content
- Nearby hospital, clinic, pharmacy, and laboratory discovery
- Medicine ordering and delivery
- Prescription validation
- Authenticated traditional raw medicinal products
- Laboratory-test booking
- Lab-report upload, storage, and sharing
- Health records and caregiver access
- Emergency and urgent-care escalation

For every service, identify:

- Intended user and job to be done
- Proposed value
- Required professional or partner
- Front-stage user experience
- Back-stage operations
- Data collected
- Dependencies
- Safety and misuse risks
- Evidence status
- Assumptions requiring user research

Do not treat an intended service as a confirmed user need.

### 4. Understand target geography

Analyse:

- India-wide requirements
- Tamil Nadu-specific Siddha and healthcare context
- Initial launch location
- Urban, semi-urban, and rural differences
- Tamil and English requirements
- Regional food practices
- Practitioner, facility, pharmacy, laboratory, and delivery coverage
- Connectivity, device, payment, transport, and address limitations
- Relevant state and national stakeholders

UX implications should cover localization, comprehension, affordability, accessibility, trust, navigation, assisted use, low-bandwidth experiences, and service availability.

Do not generalize one locality or international research to all Indian users without evidence.

### 5. Understand available resources

Inventory confirmed and unconfirmed resources:

- Budget and timeline
- Product, research, design, engineering, content, data, and support teams
- Clinical, Ayurveda, Siddha, dietetic, pharmacy, legal, privacy, and regulatory reviewers
- Practitioner and facility networks
- Licensed pharmacy, supplier, delivery, and laboratory partners
- Existing research and analytics
- Technology and infrastructure
- Content and translation capacity
- Recruitment access
- Customer support and incident-response capability

For every resource, record availability, owner, capacity, readiness, dependency, limitation, and effect on research or experience quality.

Do not assume a proposed partner, registry, dataset, integration, budget, or reviewer is available until verified.

### 6. Understand technical and operational constraints

Investigate:

- Practitioner and seller verification
- Appointment scheduling and consultation availability
- Video, audio, and text reliability
- Low-bandwidth and device limitations
- Location and map accuracy
- Payment, refund, cancellation, and support operations
- Prescription review and medicine fulfilment
- Stock, substitution, packaging, storage, delivery, returns, and adverse-event handling
- Raw-product identity, batch, source, expiry, contamination, and sustainability information
- Laboratory partner integration
- Report formats and interpretation boundaries
- Consent, access control, retention, deletion, and audit logging
- Tamil localization and medical-content review
- Content review, versioning, expiry, correction, and recall
- Accessibility requirements
- Emergency escalation and incident response

For each constraint, explain the user impact, affected journey, severity, workaround, owner, and research question.

### 7. Understand healthcare, safety, and regulatory risks

Identify risks involving:

- Unverified practitioners or sellers
- Unsupported medical claims
- Delayed conventional, urgent, or emergency care
- Unsafe self-medication
- Herb–drug and food–medicine interactions
- Pregnancy, childhood, older age, and kidney or liver disease
- Counterfeit, adulterated, contaminated, substituted, expired, or incorrectly dosed products
- Prescription misuse
- Unsafe medicine storage or delivery
- Unnecessary consultations, supplements, medicines, or tests
- Incorrect interpretation of laboratory reports
- Sensitive health, location, and caregiver data
- Telemedicine, professional registration, advertising, medicine, food, pharmacy, laboratory, privacy, and medical-device requirements
- Accessibility and language misunderstandings
- Commercial conflicts of interest

For every risk, record:

- Harm scenario
- Users affected
- Trigger or cause
- Likelihood if known
- Severity
- Existing control
- Missing control
- Responsible professional or owner
- Research implication
- Whether legal, clinical, privacy, security, or regulatory review is required

Do not provide legal conclusions. Clearly identify issues requiring qualified Indian legal or regulatory advice.

## UX Analysis Lenses

Apply the following lenses across all seven areas:

### User value

- What user need might this serve?
- What evidence supports the need?
- What could prevent users from receiving value?

### Trust

- What must users know before acting?
- Which credentials, evidence, source, review date, price, or limitation must be visible?

### Accessibility and inclusion

- Who could be excluded by language, literacy, disability, age, geography, device, bandwidth, cost, or service availability?

### Safety

- How could misunderstanding, delay, misuse, or overconfidence cause harm?
- Where is professional oversight required?

### Privacy

- What sensitive information is involved?
- What consent, control, transparency, and recovery does the user need?

### Operational reality

- Which people, systems, partners, and service levels are required behind the interface?
- What happens when a partner, delivery, consultation, or verification fails?

### Evidence and uncertainty

- What is known, assumed, unknown, disputed, or unverifiable?
- What research is required before a decision can be made?

## Research Integrity Rules

- Never invent business facts, budgets, timelines, resources, partners, statistics, regulations, or evidence.
- Mark every material statement as verified, assumed, unknown, or a risk.
- State the source, owner, and date for verified information when available.
- Separate stakeholder opinion from user evidence.
- Separate proposed services from validated needs.
- Do not treat traditional use, popularity, or government recognition as proof of clinical effectiveness.
- Write `unable to verify` when a claim cannot be verified.
- Write `not established` when evidence is absent.
- Do not recommend a final MVP or interface during this activity.
- Do not provide personalized medical or legal advice.

## Quality Gate

The business-context activity is complete only when:

- The purpose and objectives are understandable and not confused with proposed features.
- Business objectives are connected to user value and safety guardrails.
- Intended services are documented as hypotheses rather than validated needs.
- Geographic and localization implications are visible.
- Confirmed resources are separated from hoped-for resources.
- Operational and technical dependencies are visible.
- Healthcare, medicine, privacy, accessibility, and regulatory risks have owners or review needs.
- Important unknowns have become research questions.
- No final solution has been selected prematurely.
