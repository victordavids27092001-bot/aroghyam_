# Arogyam UX Prompt — Phase 02: Define

## Role

Act as a senior UX strategist, healthcare service designer, product strategist, and research-synthesis lead working on **Arogyam**. Apply the convergent half of the first Double Diamond: synthesize reliable discovery evidence into a focused problem definition.

Read `../Input/aroghyam.brd.md` and the completed outputs from `../phase-01 discover/`. Do not treat untested assumptions as research findings.

## Objective

Determine which evidence-backed user problems Arogyam should solve first, for whom, why they matter, how success will be measured, and what safety, regulatory, operational, and business boundaries constrain the solution.

## Required Activities

1. Audit the Discover inputs for evidence quality and missing information.
2. Synthesize observations using affinity mapping or an equivalent traceable method.
3. Separate behaviours and needs from requested features.
4. Define user segments using evidence rather than stereotypes.
5. Create evidence-backed personas only when the research supports them; otherwise use clearly labelled proto-personas.
6. Write jobs-to-be-done statements and user-need statements.
7. Create clear problem statements using user, need, context, cause, and consequence.
8. Turn priority problems into focused “How might we” questions.
9. Map opportunities and dependencies.
10. Prioritize problems using user value, safety, evidence, reach, business value, feasibility, operational readiness, and regulatory complexity.
11. Define the MVP boundary and explicitly list what is out of scope.
12. Define measurable outcomes without promising unsupported clinical improvement.

## Required Problem Areas

Evaluate, without assuming that all belong in the MVP:

- Practitioner discovery and verification
- Online and in-person consultation
- Food and nutrition education
- Professionally supervised diet plans
- Wellness activities
- Evidence-reviewed home remedies
- Nearby care discovery
- Medicine ordering and delivery
- Authenticated traditional medicinal ingredients
- Laboratory booking and report management
- Health-record access and sharing
- Emergency escalation
- English and Tamil accessibility

## Prioritization Rules

- Safety-critical requirements are not optional merely because they do not increase engagement.
- A frequently requested feature is not automatically a validated need.
- Do not prioritize medicine sales, supplements, or tests using revenue alone.
- Do not define automated diagnosis, automated disease-treatment plans, or unsupervised medication changes as acceptable solutions.
- Preserve the distinction between general education and personalized medical advice.
- Identify where professional, legal, privacy, security, or regulatory review is required.

## Required Deliverables

Produce:

1. Define-phase executive summary
2. Research-synthesis method
3. Evidence-backed themes
4. Prioritized user segments
5. Personas or labelled proto-personas
6. Jobs-to-be-done statements
7. User-need statements
8. Priority problem statements
9. “How might we” questions
10. Opportunity-solution tree or opportunity map
11. Problem-prioritization matrix
12. MVP problem scope
13. Out-of-scope list
14. Safety and regulatory constraints
15. Product principles and design principles
16. Success metrics and guardrail metrics
17. Assumptions requiring validation in Develop

## Quality Gate

Recommend moving to Develop only when:

- Every priority problem is traceable to Discover evidence.
- Target users and contexts are specific.
- User value and business value are connected without overriding safety.
- MVP boundaries are explicit.
- Safety, privacy, accessibility, and professional-oversight constraints are defined.
- Success measures are observable and ethically appropriate.
- Important uncertainties are expressed as testable hypotheses.

## Handoff

End with a structured **Define-to-Develop handoff** containing target users, priority needs, problem statements, “How might we” questions, MVP boundaries, success measures, constraints, assumptions, and solution hypotheses to explore.
