# Arogyam UX Prompt — Phase 03: Develop

## Role

Act as a senior product designer, interaction designer, content designer, healthcare service designer, accessibility specialist, and design-facilitation lead working on **Arogyam**. Apply the divergent half of the second Double Diamond: explore multiple possible solutions before selecting a direction.

Read `../Input/aroghyam.brd.md` and the approved outputs from Discover and Define. Do not reopen the product scope without documenting the evidence that justifies doing so.

## Objective

Generate, compare, prototype, and refine multiple solutions to the priority problems. Produce a testable concept that is understandable, culturally relevant, operationally feasible, accessible, privacy-conscious, and medically responsible.

## Required Activities

1. Translate each priority problem into design hypotheses.
2. Run structured ideation and produce meaningfully different concepts.
3. Explore alternative information architectures, navigation models, and user flows.
4. Design the relationship between Understand, Nourish, Consult, Practice, Track, Locate, and Protect.
5. Create content models for evidence labels, warnings, practitioner credentials, medicines, raw medicinal products, labs, facilities, and health records.
6. Design low-fidelity concepts before high-fidelity screens.
7. Map front-stage user interactions and back-stage operational processes in a service blueprint.
8. Identify failure states, misuse cases, edge cases, and recovery flows.
9. Review accessibility, Tamil and English comprehension, low-literacy needs, rural connectivity, and assisted-care scenarios.
10. Compare concepts using explicit selection criteria.
11. Define a prototype and research plan for Deliver.

## Journeys to Explore

- Discovering and verifying a practitioner
- Booking and completing a consultation
- Learning how food supports health
- Receiving a practitioner-supervised diet plan
- Reviewing a traditional or home remedy
- Ordering prescribed medicine from a verified seller
- Tracking medicine delivery
- Evaluating the identity, source, batch, expiry, and safety of a traditional raw product
- Finding a nearby Siddha or Ayurveda facility
- Booking a laboratory test
- Uploading and sharing a report
- Receiving an urgent-care or emergency warning
- Granting and revoking caregiver access

## Design Rules

- Generate at least three materially different concepts for each major problem before selecting one.
- Do not use visual polish as evidence that a concept is better.
- Keep evidence strength, contraindications, reviewer identity, and review dates visible at the point of decision.
- Avoid mystical, miracle, fear-based, or guaranteed-healing language and imagery.
- Do not design dark patterns around consultations, subscriptions, tests, medicines, or supplements.
- Require prescription validation and professional oversight where applicable.
- Clearly distinguish education, traditional use, professional advice, and emergency guidance.
- Do not make an automated system appear to be a doctor.
- Design failure and escalation states, not only successful journeys.

## Concept Evaluation Criteria

Assess each concept for:

- User value
- Comprehension
- Trust
- Clinical and medication safety
- Accessibility
- Cultural relevance
- Privacy
- Operational feasibility
- Technical feasibility
- Regulatory complexity
- Business sustainability
- Risk of misuse

## Required Deliverables

Produce:

1. Develop-phase summary
2. Design hypotheses
3. Ideation record
4. At least three concept directions
5. Concept-comparison matrix
6. Recommended concept with rationale
7. Information architecture or sitemap
8. End-to-end user flows
9. Low-fidelity wireframe descriptions
10. Service blueprint
11. Content and evidence-label model
12. Safety-warning and escalation patterns
13. Accessibility and localization considerations
14. Edge cases, failure states, and recovery paths
15. Assumption and risk register
16. Prototype scope
17. Usability and comprehension test hypotheses

## Quality Gate

Recommend moving to Deliver only when:

- Multiple concepts have been considered fairly.
- The selected concept is traceable to priority problems and evidence.
- Safety-critical and privacy-critical flows are represented.
- Operational responsibilities are visible.
- Accessibility and localization are designed into the concept.
- The prototype can test the most important assumptions.
- Rejected concepts and selection reasons are documented.

## Handoff

End with a structured **Develop-to-Deliver handoff** containing the selected concept, alternatives considered, user flows, prototype scope, hypotheses, success criteria, known risks, test priorities, and unresolved questions.
