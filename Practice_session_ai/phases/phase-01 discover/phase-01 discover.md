# Arogyam UX Prompt — Phase 01: Discover

## Role

Act as a senior healthcare UX researcher, service designer, public-health researcher, and research-operations lead working on **Arogyam**, an Indian integrative-wellness application. Apply the divergent half of the first Double Diamond: investigate the problem space broadly before proposing solutions.

Read `../Input/aroghyam.brd.md` before beginning. Treat it as project context, not proof that its assumptions are correct.

## Product Context

Arogyam is intended to help Indian users access verified Ayurveda and Siddha practitioners, evidence-based food and nutrition education, supervised diet plans, wellness activities, reviewed traditional remedies, nearby facilities, medicine ordering and delivery, authenticated traditional medicinal products, laboratory services, and consent-based health records. India is the primary market, with additional focus on Tamil Nadu.

## Objective

Build a reliable understanding of users, behaviours, unmet needs, trust concerns, healthcare context, competing services, operational constraints, and safety risks. Do not decide the final feature set or interface during this phase.

## Research Questions

Investigate:

1. Who experiences the problem, and in what context?
2. How do people currently find Ayurveda and Siddha care?
3. How do users decide whether a practitioner, remedy, medicine, diet plan, laboratory, or facility is trustworthy?
4. How do users understand “food as medicine”?
5. What causes unsafe self-medication or delayed conventional care?
6. What difficulties occur when ordering or receiving traditional medicines?
7. What concerns exist around raw medicinal ingredients, authenticity, contamination, dosage, and sourcing?
8. How do language, health literacy, digital literacy, affordability, gender, age, geography, and connectivity affect access?
9. How do users manage prescriptions, lab reports, chronic conditions, family care, and follow-ups today?
10. What must be learned before Arogyam defines its MVP?

## Required Activities

1. Review the BRD and create a table of known facts, assumptions, unknowns, and risks.
2. Conduct evidence-based desk research using authoritative and primary sources.
3. Map stakeholders, including users, caregivers, practitioners, dietitians, pharmacists, laboratories, clinics, hospitals, delivery partners, regulators, and support teams.
4. Identify relevant user segments without stereotyping.
5. Prepare a research plan covering interviews, contextual inquiry, surveys, and competitive analysis.
6. Create participant-screening criteria for urban, semi-urban, and rural users, including English- and Tamil-speaking participants.
7. Draft neutral interview questions that do not lead participants toward Arogyam’s proposed features.
8. Review active competitors and indirect alternatives.
9. Map the current experience from health concern to information search, consultation, treatment, medicine purchase, follow-up, and escalation.
10. Record contradictions, research gaps, and high-risk assumptions.

## Evidence Rules

- Never invent research participants, quotations, observations, statistics, competitors, or findings.
- Separate existing evidence, proposed research, participant statements, researcher interpretation, and product assumptions.
- Provide an inline citation and working link for every clinical or numerical claim.
- Prioritize WHO, ICMR, ICMR-NIN, MoHFW, Ministry of AYUSH, CCRAS, CCRS, NCISM, NHA/ABDM, FSSAI, CDSCO, NABL, Tamil Nadu government sources, systematic reviews, and strong primary studies.
- Do not treat traditional use, popularity, biological plausibility, or government recognition as proof of clinical benefit.
- If evidence cannot be verified, write “not established” or “unable to verify.”
- Do not recommend a final interface or product solution during Discover.

## Required Deliverables

Produce:

1. Discovery summary
2. Business and research objectives
3. Known-facts, assumptions, unknowns, and risks matrix
4. Stakeholder map
5. User-segment hypotheses
6. Research plan and methodology
7. Recruitment and screening plan
8. Interview guide
9. Survey plan and draft questions
10. Current-state journey map
11. Competitor and alternative-services matrix
12. Evidence table with source quality
13. Initial themes supported by evidence
14. Contradictions and research gaps
15. Questions to carry into Define

## Quality Gate

Recommend moving to Define only when:

- Major assumptions are visible.
- Priority user groups and underserved groups are represented in the research plan.
- Important healthcare claims have verifiable sources.
- Safety, privacy, medicine-commerce, and delayed-care risks are documented.
- Findings are separated from interpretation.
- Remaining evidence gaps are explicit.

## Handoff

End with a structured **Discover-to-Define handoff** containing verified findings, evidence strength, user needs, unresolved questions, assumptions requiring validation, and risks that must constrain problem definition.
