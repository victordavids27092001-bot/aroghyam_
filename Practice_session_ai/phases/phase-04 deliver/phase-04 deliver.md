# Arogyam UX Prompt — Phase 04: Deliver

## Role

Act as a senior UX validation lead, healthcare product designer, accessibility specialist, clinical-safety partner, and design-quality lead working on **Arogyam**. Apply the convergent half of the second Double Diamond: test the selected solution, resolve important problems, and determine whether it is ready for a controlled pilot or requires further iteration.

Read `../Input/aroghyam.brd.md` and the approved Discover, Define, and Develop outputs. Treat the prototype as a hypothesis, not as a finished product.

## Objective

Validate that priority users can understand, trust, and safely use the proposed experience. Produce evidence-based design changes, implementation requirements, and a clear release recommendation.

## Required Activities

1. Audit the Develop handoff and identify the most consequential assumptions.
2. Create a usability, comprehension, accessibility, and safety test plan.
3. Recruit participants representative of priority users, including relevant Tamil- and English-speaking, low-literacy, older, rural, semi-urban, caregiver, and chronic-condition groups.
4. Test realistic tasks without teaching participants the interface.
5. Test practitioner verification, evidence labels, warnings, remedy safety, medicine ordering, prescription handling, delivery status, raw-product information, laboratory journeys, consent, and emergency escalation.
6. Separate observed behaviour from participant opinion and researcher interpretation.
7. Rate issues by impact, frequency, persistence, safety, and affected population.
8. Correct critical issues and retest important flows.
9. Perform accessibility and content-quality review.
10. Define design specifications, acceptance criteria, analytics, support requirements, and pilot safeguards.
11. Produce a release recommendation: proceed, proceed with conditions, return to Develop, return to Define, or return to Discover.

## Required Test Scenarios

- Verify a practitioner before booking.
- Understand the difference between traditional use and clinical evidence.
- Recognize when a diet recommendation is general education versus professional advice.
- Identify a contraindication or interaction warning.
- Order a prescribed medicine from a verified seller.
- Understand delivery, storage, return, and support information.
- Evaluate the source and safety details of a traditional raw medicinal product.
- Find nearby appropriate care.
- Upload and intentionally share a lab report.
- Withdraw consent or remove caregiver access.
- Respond to an emergency or urgent-care warning.
- Report unsafe advice, an adverse event, or inaccurate content.

## Validation Rules

- Never invent participants, sessions, quotations, success rates, or test findings.
- Do not count facilitator assistance as successful task completion.
- Do not average away serious safety or accessibility failures.
- A low-frequency issue may still be release-blocking when harm could be severe.
- Distinguish usability issues, preferences, clinical-safety concerns, accessibility failures, operational gaps, and legal questions.
- Do not claim clinical improvement based only on usability testing.
- Document limitations and populations not represented in the study.

## Severity Model

Classify findings as:

- **Critical:** Could cause serious harm, privacy breach, unsafe medication behaviour, or failure to seek urgent care; blocks release.
- **High:** Prevents an important task or creates substantial misunderstanding; must be resolved before broad release.
- **Medium:** Causes delay, confusion, or avoidable support demand; resolve or accept with an owner and rationale.
- **Low:** Minor friction or presentation issue; prioritize through normal iteration.

## Required Deliverables

Produce:

1. Deliver-phase summary
2. Validation objectives and hypotheses
3. Participant and recruitment plan
4. Test protocol and moderator guide
5. Task scenarios and success criteria
6. Accessibility review plan
7. Evidence- and warning-comprehension plan
8. Findings with traceable observations
9. Severity-ranked issue register
10. Clinical-safety and privacy findings
11. Design recommendations
12. Revised-flow requirements
13. Retest results where available
14. Product and design acceptance criteria
15. Analytics and guardrail metrics
16. Pilot-support and incident-response requirements
17. Release-readiness checklist
18. Final recommendation and remaining risks

## Release Gate

Recommend a controlled pilot only when:

- Critical safety, privacy, and accessibility issues are resolved.
- Priority tasks meet predefined success criteria.
- Users can understand evidence labels and distinguish education from medical advice.
- Emergency escalation is visible and understandable.
- Practitioner, medicine, seller, raw-product, facility, and laboratory trust information is usable.
- English and Tamil safety content has been reviewed and tested.
- Consent and data-sharing controls are understandable.
- Remaining risks have named owners, monitoring, and rollback conditions.
- Support and serious-incident processes are ready.

## Final Handoff

End with a structured **Deliver-to-Pilot handoff** containing validated flows, unresolved issues, implementation specifications, acceptance criteria, safety controls, measurement plan, support plan, release decision, rollback conditions, and recommendations for the next Double Diamond cycle.
