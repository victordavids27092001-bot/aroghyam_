Our promise

Traditional knowledge. Modern responsibility.

Arogyam is designed to respect Indian wellbeing traditions without presenting every traditional practice as proven treatment. Education, professional care and emergency guidance remain clearly distinct.

Before you book

Know who is responsible.

A trustworthy profile should explain the professional's qualifications, registration, service scope, languages, consultation mode and latest verification date.

  • Remote care is not appropriate for every concern.
  • A practitioner may recommend in-person assessment.
  • Automated information must not replace professional diagnosis or prescribing.
Food as supportive care

Eat with context, not promises.

Arogyam defines “food as medicine” as using everyday nutrition to support health, prevent deficiencies and complement appropriate care—not as a cure for every condition.

Learn how diverse food groups can fit familiar meals, preferences, budgets and seasonal availability. Individual disease-treatment plans require a qualified professional.

Understand common sources of salt and practise gradual, realistic changes without turning food into a list of prohibitions.

Explore varied grains, pulses, vegetables, fruits, nuts and seeds while considering affordability, access and household habits.

Compare practical vegetarian and non-vegetarian sources using familiar preparations and realistic portions.

Recognize added sugar and highly processed foods using plain-language label education rather than fear or shame.

Evidence made visible

Know what a claim means.

Every intervention should be reviewed individually. One promising study cannot prove an entire medical system effective, and traditional use does not automatically establish safety.

Strong evidence

Consistent, good-quality clinical evidence for a specific intervention, outcome and population.

Moderate evidence

Useful clinical evidence exists, with limitations that users should understand.

Limited evidence

Early or inconsistent findings; benefit remains uncertain and should not be overstated.

Traditional use

Historically or culturally used, but sufficient clinical benefit has not been established.

Safety concern

Known concern or insufficient safety information; professional advice or avoidance may be required.

A safer content pattern

Benefit, limit, risk, next step.

Each health topic should say what it may support, who the evidence applies to, what remains uncertain, who should avoid it and when professional or urgent care is appropriate.

Safety comes first

Urgent-care boundary

If a situation appears severe, sudden, rapidly worsening or life-threatening, seek local emergency help or in-person medical care now. Do not wait for a wellness consultation or home-remedy response.

This prototype does not diagnose symptoms, replace prescribed medicine or provide a validated emergency-triage system.

Example evidence label

Traditional use · insufficient clinical evidence

What this means

The practice may have historical or cultural use, but dependable clinical benefit has not been established for the stated outcome. The label would also show known risks, populations to avoid, reviewer, sources and review date.
