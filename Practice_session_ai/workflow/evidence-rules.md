# Evidence Rules

## Evidence Classes

1. Verified factual source
2. User research evidence
3. Direct observation
4. Expert opinion
5. Clinical evidence
6. Regulatory or policy requirement
7. Analyst interpretation
8. Product hypothesis
9. Unverified assumption

Every important finding must identify its class and source.

## Source Rules

- Prefer Indian government, WHO, ICMR–NIN, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Ministry of AYUSH, NCISM, NHA/ABDM, FSSAI, CDSCO, NABL, Tamil Nadu government, systematic reviews, and strong peer-reviewed studies.
- Open every cited source and use the original document when available.
- Record publication year, measured year, population, sample size where available, geography, and access date for numerical claims.
- Do not use promotional clinics, supplement sellers, or wellness blogs as evidence of medical effectiveness.
- Mark unavailable or conflicting evidence explicitly.

## Interpretation Rules

- Association is not causation.
- Qualitative frequency is not population prevalence.
- User belief is not clinical proof.
- Expert opinion is not user evidence.
- Traditional use is not proof of safety or effectiveness.
- Lack of reported harm is not proof of safety.
- Recommendations must not exceed evidence strength.

## Audit Fields

For each significant claim record:

- Claim ID
- Claim or decision
- Source and link
- Evidence class
- Geography and population
- Confidence
- Limitation
- Reviewer
- Review date
